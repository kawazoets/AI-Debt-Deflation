# Chapter 10 — assessment as of 2026-11-25
Scope: United States. Qualitative framework, not an empirically calibrated crisis probability or a global forecast.

## Verified current references
- Federal Reserve Financial Accounts, 2026 Q2: household debt / disposable personal income 0.90; near lowest since late 1990s excluding pandemic income effects. https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/recent_developments.htm
- Federal Reserve Financial Stability Report, November 2026: combined private nonfinancial debt grew more slowly than GDP. https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20261113.pdf
- BLS productivity release November 5, 2026: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/prod2_11052026.htm

## Monitoring indicators
- Employee compensation / household debt: annualized nominal flow divided by nominal stock.
- Discretionary consumption / disposable income: requires explicit PCE category definition; not yet calculated.
- Business internal funds / nonfinancial business debt: match sector scope and annualize quarterly flows; not accounting operating cash flow.
- New loan originations / outstanding loan balances: match loan category, population and reporting period; not yet calculated.
- Debt service / nominal income: use official DSR and household distribution evidence.

## Evidence stages
1. Productivity improvements: observed, attribution to AI not established.
2. Occupational labor demand weakness: partial evidence, AI causal attribution uncertain.
3. Broad persistent AI-induced income loss among debtors: not established.
4. AI-induced forced sales and credit contraction feedback: not established.
5. Self-reinforcing debt deflation: not established.

No claim that a systemic crisis is imminent. Earlier scenario thresholds are illustrative, not calibrated to actual US economy. No new Python simulation in this chapter.
